THE DEEP

A lit cabin with Mikey at the glass, looking out into black water where the real work swims: everything is dark and silent by default, and the only thing that ever happens is something rising into the light.

The argument, one sentence: work you aren't watching isn't a board you forgot to check — it's dark water, so the honest interface is one whose resting state is nothing visible, where depth means distance from you, and where "needs you" is not a badge but a thing physically ascending toward the surface until you deal with it.

depth = how far a thread is from wanting you silence = darkness, not an empty list spend = hull pressure settle = it sinks and dims pure CSS motion · no script
01

Desktop main window — at rest, with work in flight

The whole window is one porthole. Vertical position is the only ranking that exists: at the surface = wants you now; shallows = moving, will finish without you; deep = long-running and deliberately silent; trench = watchers and settled work sinking out of memory. Nothing here can be dragged between lanes because there are no lanes — the water mirrors the spine, it never becomes one. At a glance you read one number: how much has come near the top.

05

Phone — deliberately thin: a face in the dark and a key to hold

One screen. No information architecture, no ported desktop. Mikey's face lit in black water, one caption of whatever he last said, a depth ribbon of six dots so you know the shape of the day without reading anything, and a push-to-talk ring. Tapping a dot doesn't open a screen — it makes him tell you about it. That's the phone: he's the UI, the water is a rumour.

What the phone deliberately does not have

No water view you can pan. No transcript archive. No ticket list, no filters, no settings beyond the device toggle. If you need to see something, you ask and he reads it — and if it truly needs eyes, he says “that one's a desktop thing.” Everything above is one column and could ship in a week.

The one phone-native idea worth keeping

The ribbon is ambient, not interactive-first. Six dots at a glance from a lock screen widget tell you whether anything broke surface while you were out. That is 90% of what the phone is for.

06

Decisions

ChoseRejectedTradeoff, one line
One vertical axis: depth = distance from wanting you Columns, lanes, a graph of dependencies You lose the ability to see relationships between threads; you gain a window whose single glance answers the only question you actually ask ("does anything need me?").
Darkness as the resting state A populated dashboard that always looks busy An idle room looks like almost nothing is there — which is honest and calming, but means the design must earn attention on the rare occasions it asks for it.
Spend as hull pressure (ambient bar + a marker for "a frontier brain is out") A cost table with per-call rows Not auditable at a glance — but the audit log already exists in the spine; the UI's job is to make expensive turns feel expensive.
Settled work sinks and dims instead of disappearing Archive tab / "done" column Costs a little screen real estate at the bottom; buys the felt sense that the room has history and that finishing is a physical event.
Open-in-place only — no second page anywhere Detail routes, drill-downs, a thread inspector window Deep content (long diffs) is cramped inside the panel; you never lose your spatial memory of the room, which is the whole reason the metaphor works.
"Keep me posted" = a violet tether + an eyed tag A live/call mode with its own screen Subscriptions are only discoverable by speech, so you must teach the phrase once; in exchange there is no mode to be stuck in and exactly one way out ("stand down").
At most two faces; the second one has a visible burning fuse A cast of persona cards, a room of avatars Loses the charm of the full TMNT lineup on screen; keeps voices from becoming theatre and makes "mortal" legible instead of a doc footnote.
One-offs are white sparks that visibly die Showing untracked work in the same list as tickets A little visual noise near the surface; makes the two-flow architecture (ticket / no ticket) something you can see rather than something you must remember.
The slate (transcript) is a fixed third column, not a chat app Chat as the primary surface Long conversations get cramped; the water stays the hero and the transcript stays what it is — a receipt, not the product.
07

Cost of building it

new engineeringThe one line

Everything visual here is CSS over the existing snapshot — the genuinely new backend work is three things: (1) a depth score per thread the daemon computes and ships in PanelSnapshot (surface / shallows / deep / trench, derived from the spine's ticket state + last operator-gate event + silence duration) — this is the salience filter with a number attached, and it must live in the daemon since clients only render; (2) a per-thread narration subscription (set by speech, stored server-side, expires) that routes one thread's progress events through Mikey instead of the silent log; and (3) a streaming terminal projection per thread so the expanded panel can show a live feed without giving the UI its own tail process. Everything else — bobbing, snow, tethers, the sinking of settled work — is styling of data the snapshot already carries or will carry once (1) exists.

08

What I'd cut if you told me to rein it in

In order. Each cut keeps the concept; only the last one would kill it.

1. The continuous water simulation

Marine snow, caustics, light shaft, per-lantern bob phases, sinking settle animations. Beautiful, and the most GPU and fiddly-tuning per pixel of anything here.

cheap versionStatic gradient, four fixed depth bands, lights that only animate when their state changes. Keeps the metaphor and 80% of the feeling for roughly a day of work.

2. The live terminal feed inside the expanded panel

Real streaming projection per thread is new daemon plumbing plus back-pressure and a scrollback policy.

cheap versionLast 20 lines, polled on the snapshot, with a "tail in Terminal" button. The facet still exists; it just isn't live.

3. Free positioning inside a depth band

Horizontal placement, collision avoidance and stable identity across snapshots is real layout work for zero information gain.

cheap versionFixed slots — a lantern always sits in the same column within its band. Uglier, more stable, and arguably easier to learn.

4. The dock as a live cross-section

A second animated realm running all day for a 100px strip.

cheap versionMikey's face, one amber dot, one count. Still alive, still glanceable, one tenth the code.

Do not cut: the depth axis itself

If threads stop being ranked by "how close to wanting you", the water becomes decoration on a list and this board becomes the thing round 1 was rejected for.

the lineDepth must mean something computed, or don't build it.
