Decisions

ChoseRejectedThe tradeoff in one line
Feed channels on a bridge — work as horizontal instruments you open in place A star-map constellation Instruments give more truth per pixel; motion supplies the sci-fi, not the metaphor.
Raw stdout as the hero surface, typographically staged Summaries-only with logs buried in a drawer The live terminal is the owner's explicit ask; Mikey's narration carries comprehension so the feed doesn't have to.
Every readout maps to a real counter (spend, chars, tier, queue depth, Δ-progress) Decorative dials, radar sweeps, fake load graphs Fewer instruments, all trustworthy — cockpit cosplay with invented telemetry is worse than honest text.
Expansion in place (disclosure channels; stdout/transcript/diff/spend inside) Drill-down pages or a detail pane router The room stays one continuous place; the cost is vertical space when several channels are open at once.
“Wants you” = hot channel edge + a held sentence Notification popups / focus-stealing modals Nothing interrupts; the risk of missing it is covered by the dock LED and Mikey's voice at the next lull.
Mikey full-size at the station, watch-chip and salience queue attached to him Avatar as a corner chip beside the “real” UI He is the interpreter, so he gets interpreter real estate — worth the ~250px column it costs the feeds.

What this buys, what I'd cut

Real new engineering, one line: a per-thread live tail fanout in the daemon — generalize live-tail.ts into a per-channel stdout/transcript stream on the existing panel WS — plus spend and last-progress counters folded into PanelSnapshot; every other pixel here renders from the snapshot clients already receive.
If you told me to rein it in, I'd cut, in order:
  1. Live streaming stdout (the tail fanout above) → cheap version: the last 12 lines of the thread transcript, refreshed with each snapshot, fetched only while a channel is expanded. You lose the crawl, you keep the truth.
  2. Animated spend odometers and per-utterance cost chips → cheap version: static numbers in the masters rail updated per snapshot; per-utterance cost stays in the replay sidecar JSON where it already lives.
  3. Per-channel heat / time-since-progress instrumentation (needs progress events from threads) → cheap version: the mtime of the thread's transcript file, rendered coarsely as active / quiet > 5m — one stat call, no thread cooperation needed.
What survives every cut: Mikey at the station with the real frames animating, channels that open in place, the hot edge for “wants you”, and a masters rail whose numbers are never fictional.

constraints honored: no <script> · no forms · no iframes · no inline handlers · no external requests · all motion is CSS keyframes/steps() · prefers-reduced-motion freezes everything and the page still reads
