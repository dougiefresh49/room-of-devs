Round C · concept board · opus

The Settle Line
one column, three crops

Every surface is the same single column cropped to fit; one horizontal rule across it decides what earns the room's only audio floor; and one material rule — hatched is mortal, solid is settled — makes #73's whole doctrine visible without reading a word.

PRIMARY OBJECT: the ticket (row) · the thread is a sprig hanging off it NEW BACKEND: one policy file, one queue file, one lull timer

00The reading

This is not a room. It's a watch station.

The scarce resource in this product is not screen space, agents, or tickets — it is the owner's attention and the one audio floor. Everything else is already durable somewhere else (GitHub, the repo, transcripts). So the UI's job is not to hold state; it is to decide what crosses into you, and to make that decision inspectable and editable.

That gives the whole design its spine: a vertical column where height means audibility. Above the line, you hear it. On the line is the policy. Below the line, you can see it if you look. Below the second line it is only written down. The column is the salience surface — not a settings screen you visit, but the thing you stare at all day.

Why one column on all three surfaces

The brief asks whether the surfaces should even share a shape. My answer is a hard yes, and it is the cheapest good decision on this board: the panel, the dock and the phone render the same ordered column, differing only in how much of it is cropped and whether a detail pane sits beside it.

The dock is not a different app — it is the top of the column: the floor, the speak line, and flagged rows only. The phone is the column, full bleed. The panel is the column plus a detail pane. Two JS realms, one mental model, one place for a bug to live.

Why the ticket is the row and the thread is a sprig

I accept the brief's mortality argument, and then I go further than accepting it — I draw it. The row is the ticket: a monospace number, immortal, the join key for everything. Hanging under it, indented and hatched, is the thread currently serving it.

When the thread settles, the sprig vanishes and is replaced by a solid green settle-note with the commit and the conclusion. Nothing about the row moved. A user who has watched that happen twice has internalised the architecture, and no onboarding copy was required.

The one material rule

Hatched = mortal

Threads, workers, checked-out voices, unkept artifacts, estimated spend. It exists now and it is going to die. Never trust it as a record.

Solid = settled

Tickets, settle-notes, kept artifacts, billed cost. It survived the context window. This is the spine, and you can cite it.

Blue gutter = wants you

The only chromatic alarm in the product. Amber means grinding, grey means quiet, green means done. If nothing is blue, you are not needed.

03The dock — a non-activating NSPanel

The dock is the top of the column. That's all it ever is.

Because the panel must never steal focus, it contains no text input and no scroll region — a text field needs key focus, and key focus is activation. Every control is a single click on a big target. Attachments are exempt here for the same reason: the dock is not a reply surface, so input parity does not apply to it. Reply lives one click away ("open" hands off to the main window, which is allowed to activate).

Held items coalesce: when the lull comes, Mikey speaks one sentence covering all three, not three clips. Three held milestones cost one Gemini call and one synthesis. Dropping is free and silent — the events are still in the log tail.

Why the queue is a pill and not a panel

A queue with its own screen invites you to manage it. This one lives as a pill welded to the speak line, because the correct number is zero and the correct interaction is usually none. If it grows past ~6 the pill turns amber — that is not a backlog, it is a policy bug, and the fix is one row in the table above.

Silence is designed, not absent. When the queue drains to empty the pill reads "queue empty" in grey and stays. The room reporting that it has nothing to say is a real message, and the only way to trust silence is to be shown that it was chosen.

one-off light full

A three-bar gauge glyph, 9px tall, on every row that has a thread. It is set once at spawn (a toggle-group in the confirm step) and afterwards it is read-only — changing ceremony mid-thread is a different thread, and the UI refuses to pretend otherwise.

07Decisions

What I chose, what I refused, and what it costs

AreaChoseRejectedTradeoff
Surface shape One vertical column, identical ordering, on all three surfaces; panel adds a detail pane, dock crops to the top Purpose-built shapes per surface (board on desktop, feed on phone) Gives up desktop-specific density and the two-pane overview a wide screen could afford; buys one component tree, one mental model, and a dock that is provably consistent with the window.
Primary object The ticket, as a row. The thread is a hatched sprig hanging under it that disappears on settle. Session/agent cards; a pure event feed; "the day" A ticketless one-off has no row until it graduates, so quick asks live only in the floor and the log — acceptable, since that is exactly their lifespan.
Material language Hatched = mortal/provisional, solid = settled/durable. Applied to threads, artifacts, spend estimates, checked-out voices. Color-coding mortality; badges reading "ephemeral" Hatching is subtle at small sizes and needs a legend once; buys a single rule that teaches the architecture instead of nine labels that describe it.
Salience A horizontal Speak Line that is both the divider and the button; policy is a 9-row table of event classes × speak/hold/log Drag an event class across the line; per-thread mute toggles; a notification-center screen Direct manipulation would be more satisfying and drifts toward anti-goal 7; a table is diffable, checked in, and reviewable by a human at 3am.

Cost readout Ambient hairline per row + explicit chips only for escalations, each naming the config rule that fired; hatched = estimate, solid = billed A global spend meter in chrome; a cost dashboard; per-turn pricing on every message No single "today's burn" number at a glance (it lives in settings); buys attention on the only spend that is ever surprising — the escalations.

Work-state editing The ledger is strictly read-only. State changes go through the concierge or an "open on GitHub" link. Inline state menus, drag between states, close/assign from the row Two clicks instead of one for a state change; buys total immunity to becoming an orchestrator runtime, and keeps the tracker unambiguously the writer.
Rooms One merged column with a 2-char monospace room tag; room switcher only filters Tabs or a sidebar per room; separate columns Two busy rooms will make the column noisy; buys correct behaviour for the shared audio floor — which is global — and costs nothing in v1's single room.

Copy doctrine The concierge speaks in the second person about work, cites ticket numbers inline, and never narrates itself ("I'm looking into that") Persona catchphrases in status text; progress narration as personality Less character in text; buys character concentrated where it is actually free — the voice — and text that stays scannable.

08Priced inventions

Three small files and a timer. That is the whole bill.

  1. Salience policy + held queue. A checked-in salience.json (event class → speak/hold/log) read by the existing queue watcher, plus state/salience_queue.json for held items, surfaced in PanelSnapshot. COST: ~1 day. One new state file, one new snapshot field, one filter in the ingest path. No new process.
  2. Lull detection. "No playback and no PTT for 90s" — a timer over .now-playing.json and the grant state that already exist. Drains and coalesces the held queue into one Gemini call and one synthesis. COST: ~2 hours. Strictly cheaper than today: N announcements become 1.
  3. Ticket projection into the snapshot. A poller that turns gh issue list plus machine-readable state labels into the ledger rows, cached to state/spine.json. COST: ~1 day, and it is already the #73 build item ("spine validation experiment"). Not new work, just newly rendered.
  4. Spend log tail. The interpreter already emits one log line per LLM call; make it JSONL keyed by ticket, tail it into the snapshot for the hairlines and the escalation chips. COST: ~half a day. No new billing integration; ElevenLabs credits already have a fetcher sitting unused.
  5. Artifacts directory. Watch artifacts/ for the mermaid→SVG output this repo's docs-publish path already produces; "keep" is a git mv plus a commit. COST: ~half a day, reusing an existing renderer.
  6. Explicitly NOT invented: no timing capture, no seek engine, no canvas, no stroke model, no floor/turn machinery, no database, no new orchestrator runtime, no always-on transcription, no per-agent presence protocol. COST: 0. This is the point.

The honest risk: the ledger is only as good as the spine's state labels. If needs-feedback stays prose, the blue gutter is a lie. This board therefore hard-depends on the spine validation experiment landing first — which the architecture docs already sequence as the first build item. I would rather this dependency be loud than designed around.

09What today's UI should lose

  • The persona grid (RoomGrid, AgentCard on both platforms). It is the cancelled premise rendered directly. Personas survive as voices and as one dock face.
  • Per-session cards as a navigable object. Sessions become the hatched sprig under a ticket, and they are not clickable — there is nothing durable to open.
  • CallView as a separate mode. "Live/call" was a mode because narration was all-or-nothing. With a salience policy, a call is just milestone → speak for one thread, expressed as one toggle on the work item. One screen fewer, one state machine fewer.
  • The hidden-devs / picker sheet. Rooms are a manifest; who exists is config, not a UI you browse.
  • Replay history as a primary surface. It becomes "replay" on the floor and the log tail. Nobody scrolls an audio history; they ask.
  • Every state badge except four. wants-you / grinding / watching / settled. If a distinction cannot survive being read from four feet, it belongs in the detail pane.

10Anti-goal self-check

PASS1 · not a chatbot — the ledger is the app; the conversation is one strip at the top and one composer at the bottom.
PASS2 · no multi-voice theater — zero avatars in the window, one in the dock, and checkout replaces rather than adds.
PASS3 · no real-time ink — artifacts are files with version tabs; there is no canvas anywhere on this board.
PASS4 · no lipsync escalation — lipsync lives only inside the dock avatar leaf, mutated by ref, driving nothing.
PASS5 · no always-listening mic — hold-to-talk on all three surfaces, with the "nothing captured before the press" line printed in the UI.
PASS6 · no resident brain — the floor line cites ticket numbers, and "why this spoke" points at the salience rule, not a memory.
NEAR7 · no new orchestrator runtime — see below.
PASS8 · no client conversation state — every region here is a projection of one snapshot; the column holds selection and scroll, nothing else.
PASS9 · no database — three JSON files and a git repo. The ledger's sort order is computable from the snapshot in one pass.
PASS10 · no silent capability growth — saving a verb is a named confirm step, and saved verbs appear as files in the room manifest.

Closest to violating: #7, "no new orchestrator runtime." A column of tickets sorted by state, with a ceremony dial and inline actions, is one interaction away from being a tracker. I held the line three ways, and they are all load-bearing: the ledger is read-only (no inline state menus, no drag, no close/assign); ceremony is spawn-time only and read-only afterwards; and every write path is either the concierge or a link out to GitHub. If a future round adds "just let me close it from the row," this board becomes the thing #73 rejected — so treat that as the tripwire.

Runner-up: #2. Two windows means two potential faces. The rule that keeps it honest is that checkout replaces the concierge everywhere rather than joining it, so the maximum is one face at rest and two only during the transition animation.
