Round C concept board · board-fable · drawn blind

THE QUIET FLOOR — operations, whispered

The room is working correctly when it is silent — so every surface is an attention ledger that makes silence legible and interruptions earned, work is text you glance at, and the only faces in the building belong to voices.

01

The reading

The old app was a theater: a grid of characters, each with a face, each entitled to speak. The picked architecture inverts that — the spine is the mind, threads are mortal, and exactly one voice fronts everything. So this board's strong opinion: stop drawing agents. A thread is not a character; it's a line item on a ledger. Faces are reserved for the two things that can actually talk to you — the concierge, and (rarely, temporarily) a checked-out walkthrough voice. Everything else is typography.

Primary object: the work item — ticket + thread + activity + artifacts + spend. I accept the brief's mortality argument, but I sharpen it: the work item is the primary noun, while the primary axis is attention. The main view is not a board of tickets grouped by tracker state — it's a ledger grouped by what the room wants from you: Needs you → Holding for a lull → Working → Settled today. Tracker state is a chip on the row; attention state is the layout. That's the salience filter made physical — the reason this round exists gets the whole left column, not a settings pane.

The three surfaces get three different jobs, one shared material. The panel is the floor: full ledger + inspector + the concierge's desk. The dock is a one-line pager: the quiet line, and nothing else until something earns a pixel. The phone is the same ledger folded to one column with a thumb-sized push-to-talk. No surface is a chat app; the conversation is a bar at the bottom of a room that is mostly about work you can see.

What today's UI loses

  • The persona card grid. Cards keyed to sessions die at /clear; rows keyed to tickets don't.
  • The dock avatar pill. A row of faces implies a row of speakers. The dock becomes a status line.
  • Per-session PTT targets. You talk to the room; the interpreter routes (talk-back binds to a ticket). One press, one grant path, unchanged usePttGrant.
  • The five-button action cluster. Replaced by three verbs that survive the recut: speak it, reply, walk-me-through.
  • Character swap per session. Voices belong to the room's cast (a manifest), not to threads.

What this board makes the daemon do that it doesn't do today. No timing capture, no seek engine, no canvas, no database — the expensive subsystems are the ones this design refuses to need.

08

Self-check against the ten anti-goals

1 voice+KB chatbot — pass: conversation is a bar; the ledger dominates every surface and answers cite tickets. 2 multi-voice theater — pass by construction: threads have no faces, so two avatars (Mikey + a dashed-ring checkout) is the physical maximum. 3 real-time ink — pass: artifacts are rendered files with version chips; no canvas exists to draw on. 4 lipsync escalation — pass: one 38px <img> ref at the desk; lipsync drives nothing. 5 always-listening mic — pass: every capture is a held press (⌥space, dock dot, thumb bar), released to send. 6 resident brain — pass: the borrow state literally narrates its own mortality (“fresh context… then it dies”), and answers carry ticket citations. 8 client state — pass: everything shown is snapshot-derived; hold queue, ledger, and routing table are daemon files. 9 database — pass: three new files, zero indexes. 10 silent capability growth — pass: saved verbs would surface as an offered, named confirmation at the desk (same pattern as “Keep → commit”).

The one I came closest to violating: #7 — the work surface mirrors the tracker, it never becomes one. The Needs-you card's verbs sit one careless build away from tracker verbs: “Approve” is a routed reply that a thread interprets, but a lazy implementation would wire it straight to an issue-state transition, and then the ledger is an orchestrator UI with drag-to-transition a sprint away. The guardrail is drawn into the design: work rows carry exactly three verbs — speak, reply, walkthrough — all of which route through the concierge to a thread; no element on any surface writes tracker state directly, and anything heavier is the “Open #74 ↗” escape hatch to GitHub itself.
