Two faces, one stage — the checked-out voice

state 6 · never a group call

The rule is that voices only ever talk to you, and one of them is temporary. So they never share eye level. Checking out a voice is a stage handoff: Mikey steps back into a small, grey, animation-frozen standby figure at the edge of the field, Donnie takes the well, and a dashed violet leash plus a draining lease bar say plainly that this is borrowed and mortal. When it drains, Donnie leaves a written note on the ticket and vanishes; Mikey warms back up and tells you what landed.

One face, one caption, one "where we are" strip, one physical hold-to-talk target. That's the whole phone this round.

The strip is the desktop field flattened to a single axis: how far the room has come, and which band the live work sits in. It is not a list and it does not scroll.

Explicitly not built here: any browsing, any list of threads, any second screen. If you need to see inside something, you ask him and he tells you — the phone is the voice interface with a face on it.

Design debt taken on purpose. The desktop is where this round's ambition went.

Decisions

chose / rejected / tradeoff
ChoseRejectedTradeoff
Face at the centre; work orbits itAvatar in a rail beside a list You can't see forty items at once. You shouldn't — that's what the spine and GitHub are for.
Radius = closeness to needing youColumns or lanes by ticket status "Which is the 4th most urgent" is unanswerable by construction. Only "does this want me" is legible.
The salience threshold drawn as a ring you can dragA notification-preferences pane The anti-chatterbox rule becomes a physical object you tune in one gesture — but the daemon now has to score events, not just classify them.
One easel he holds things up on, with a stack behind itFree-floating artifact cards / tabs Only one thing is shown at a time. Comparing two diffs means the stack, not a split view.
Motes expand in place; the room pauses while you lookDrill-down to a detail page Deep content lives in a 330px popover; anything bigger has to graduate to the easel.
"Keep me posted" = a visible tether from his eyelineA separate live/call mode Live stops being a mode and becomes a property of one thread. Two or three tethers is the readable limit.
Spend as heat, colour and an emberA cost table in a corner You feel a frontier turn instead of reading it; exact numbers move one expand away (mote peek, ticket total).
Checkout = stage handoff, loser goes grey and freezesTwo faces side by side You cannot address both at once — which is correct, they never talk to each other anyway.
Caption ribbon (last thing said)A scrolling chat transcript column Scrollback is one expand away instead of always-on. This is a room, not an inbox.
Ceremony gear = concentric rings on the moteA "process" label or badge Reads instantly, teaches slowly — the first week you have to be told that three rings means full gates.

What this actually buys

new engineering · and the rein-in

Needs real new engineering

A salience score + threshold policy in the daemon (each event gets a rank and a speak / defer-to-lull / log-only verdict, so the ring means something), and a field projection in PanelSnapshot — per live thread: radius, state, gear, watched-flag, spend-so-far. Everything else on this board is rendering over data the room already has.

If you told me to rein it in

  • The continuous attention field (radius as a live scalar, motes drifting inward as urgency rises) is the expensive one — it needs scoring, smoothing and layout that never collides. Cheap version: three fixed bands — rim / mid / his hand — and items snap between them on the four operator-gate events we already emit. Same picture, no physics, no scoring.
  • The easel as a general showing surface (terminal feed, diff, rendered diagram, versioned artifacts, a stack). Cheap version: plan-approval and transcript only; a "shown" file opens in the editor instead of rendering in-panel, and the terminal feed is the existing live-tail as plain monospace text.
  • The checkout stage handoff (Mikey physically stepping back, leash, draining lease). Cheap version: keep Donnie's face pinned small at the top of the easel with a "temporary" ring and a settles-to-ticket line. Costs one component, keeps the whole meaning.
  • The motion budget — sweep, orbits, dust, ripples. Cheap version: keep breathing, blink, lipsync and the arrival ripple; delete the radar sweep and the orbiting workers. That's ~80% of the aliveness for a static field.
