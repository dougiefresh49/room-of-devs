The Reused Room
eight forks, decided

Nothing here is invented. Every element comes from one of the four final boards, cited. The four agree on almost everything, so the work of a merge isn't picking a winner — it's settling the eight places they contradict each other and refusing to ship both answers. Where three boards say one thing and one says another, the minority sometimes wins: the cast rail never reorders, and that call comes from the shortest board.

00 · the model

Three nouns, unchanged

The Floor

Exactly one voice, ever. Who has it, who's queued, who's blocked. A huddle is the same floor passed among more people. all four

The Tape

Every turn — voice, ink, object, outcome — as one record on one timeline. Replay, catch-up and history are all seek. 1F1T1T · Den

The Table

One shared surface holding one object. Glance, sketch, paste and huddle stop being four features. Workbench · Den · 1F1T1T

assembly ruleWhere the boards agree, adopt verbatim and move on. Where they conflict, pick one and write down the loser. Where all four are silent — say so out loud rather than inventing, and hand it to concept B.

02 · the state model

Six places to stand, and a politeness ladder

the rule with teethNo earcon ever plays over a voice — they queue behind speech and collapse (three throat-clears become one). An agent never asks twice at the same volume. That is the audio meaning of "waits politely," and it is the difference between a room you keep the volume up on and one you mute in week two.

Three volumes of the same room, one dial: out loud (speakers, talk or type) · headphones (same audio, mic off, composer primary) · silent (nobody speaks; the karaoke line becomes a reading strip; everything lands on the tape as unheard stripes). This is what makes typed replies structural rather than a fallback. 1F1T1T · Den

05 · screen awareness

The Glance — inspectable before, during and after

The gesture and expiry come from the Table boards; the four-stage rigour comes from the Workbench, which had the most complete consent model of the four. A glance is one frame you deliberately hand over, and like turning a laptop toward a coworker, it ends when you turn it back.

1 · TOSS

Flick the dock up

Or say "look at this" while holding a card, or ⌥⇧G. One frame of the focused window. A shutter tick marks the moment. Nothing is sent yet.

2 · REDACT

3s scrim + brush

Paint out secrets before the frame leaves the machine. Silence sends; Esc means it never happened. The audience is named — "Show Donnie", not "share screen."

3 · LIVE

Green frame on the real screen edge

The frame is the permission indicator, not a notification about one. Countdown visible. He can mark it up and talk about it.

4 · AFTER

Pixels expire, record doesn't

5 minutes, then gone from the table and from his context. The tape keeps ◱ glanced at Cursor → Donnie · 14s forever, so "what exactly did he see?" always has an answer.

fork settled hereGreen (Den) over violet (F&T · Workbench) and amber (1F1T1T) · the Workbench's four-stage model over the others' three · the Den's agent-initiated ask added as a fifth entry point.

06 · the baton

One object for talking back

Adopted whole from One Floor, One Table, One Tape — the merge that makes "typed replies and attachments everywhere" one component instead of a PTT key plus a composer. It exists at three sizes with one muscle memory: hold to arm (~600ms ring fill, so no accidental hot mics), speak or type, release to send, drag off to cancel.

🎙 Speak

Room ducks to 20% within 120ms and stops the current speaker at the next clause boundary, never mid-word.

⌨ Type

Start typing instead of holding. Same turn, same tape. In silent mode it's the only channel — which is why it isn't a fallback.

📎 Attach · ◱ Show

⌘V or drag; or attach a glance to this turn so the picture and the sentence arrive together. Everything lands on the table, addressed.

Aiming is physical, not navigational. Hold a seat, press ⌘1–6, or say a name. Hold with nobody aimed and you're addressing the room — that's how you give a voice command, and how you open a huddle.

11 · honesty

What this merge cannot fix

Three problems are structural to all four sources, so no amount of merging touches them. They're the brief for concept B.

  • The Table is empty on day one. Glance, napkin, huddle and paste are all unbuilt, so the centre of the primary window currently holds the last spoken summary and a shelf with nothing on it. Every board justifies the Table by the futures it enables; none says what it shows before they arrive.
  • Ink timed to speech is unpriced. Word-level timestamps, clause anchors surviving the Gemini rewrite, and a canvas replay engine bound to the audio clock — presented as a motion budget, delivered as a subsystem. It is the likeliest thing on all four boards to be quietly dropped.
  • Cutting in is designed as an audio operation. Duck to 20%, stop at a clause boundary. That's the speaker. Nothing in any of the four says whether your sentence reaches the agent in time to change what it does.

Where this concept is strongest, it's because four models agreed. Where it's weakest, it's because four models agreed.
