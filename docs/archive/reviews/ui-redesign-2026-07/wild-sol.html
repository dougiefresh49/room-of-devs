Room of Devs / concept 01

The room is a radio play.

Not four chat windows. One living soundtrack—cast, paced, and glanceable enough to disappear behind the work.

Agents speak in character. The interface behaves like stage direction: who has the floor, what comes next, and where your attention is needed.

00 / THESIS

Design a soundstage, not a status board.

The room has a floor, a queue, and a score. One agent speaks at a time. Finished work enters the show; blockers wait off-mic with a visible hand raised. The developer conducts rather than monitors.

04 / AUDIO GRAMMAR

Attention is choreographed.

Not every completion deserves an interruption. The room’s audio grammar uses character, urgency, and time to decide what becomes speech.

01

Earcon

A character-specific two-note cue identifies who is entering before the first word.

Identity without looking
02

Headline

The agent leads with outcome and risk. Details stay in the visible score unless asked for.

Seven seconds first
03

The floor

Only one agent speaks. Simultaneous finishes wait in the wings instead of piling up.

Serial, never cacophony
04

Raised hand

Questions wait politely, then escalate from a visual notch to a spoken interruption.

Urgency earns volume
The memorable object is not an avatar. It is the feeling that your agents know when to speak.
Why this direction
01

Audio becomes architecture.

Floor, wings, score, and raised hands make the invisible sound system legible without turning it into chat.

02

Characters carry recognition, not decoration.

Color, initials, entry cues, and voice are enough. Lip-sync frames can add life without asking the interface to become a cartoon.

03

Each surface serves a different distance.

The Stage explains, the Periscope reassures, and the Walkie lets you act. None is a shrunken copy of another.

04

Voice commands stay trustworthy.

Turning multi-step commands into a visible score gives the developer a fast way to audit what the room heard and what it will do next.
