Room of Devs · synthesis board · candidate for the locked target

The Night Booth A warm late-night studio built from channel strips and tally lamps — where exactly one voice is on air, and light means sound.

Thesis. Every surface answers the same question first: who is talking, and what did they just say? So the room is a console of full-width channel strips sorted by state — not a grid of equal tiles — inside a warm charcoal studio that can sit in a dev's periphery all night. State is always a lamp plus a monospace word, never color alone; personas are jerseys, not themes (ring, edge bar, name); and ON AIR wins by light, not hue — the one achromatic glow allowed on screen, so it can never collide with Raph's red or Donnie's purple. The mic is one talkback affordance everywhere, and every voice command comes back as a receipt: what I heard, what I did, what it cost.

Lamps for states, jerseys for personas — in a warm room

System sans for anything a human wrote or an agent said; ui-monospace for anything the machine knows — state, timers, model, path, counts. The split is the studio's console voice: you can tell speech from telemetry without reading.

Spacing, radius & motion

4px base. Strip padding 11/12, 8 between strips, 14/16 pane padding, 22 on phone sheets. Radius is equipment-grade: 6 controls, 10 strips, 14 panes, 20 only where iOS demands it; 999 reserved for lamps and the round talkback key. Rounder = more physical = touch it.

Motion budget: lamps breathe, the on-air meter animates, the grant ring fills — nothing else moves. The avatar's 70ms frame swap is the fastest thing on screen and never competes.

The avatar contract

Every avatar is a bare square <img> in a round mask. The persona ring, state lamp, and on-air glow are all sibling elements drawn by the parent — no filters, transitions, or React state on the image — so the 70ms lip-sync remains one img.src write outside the render tree.

Sizes: 44 panel strip, 40 phone strip, 52 dock, 132 call screen. Placeholders here are initials-in-a-disc.

07 Rationale

The contested calls, decided

ON AIR is light, not hue

Two boards used a hue for speaking (amber, teal/green); both eventually collide — amber sits next to Mikey's orange and any "hand up" warm tone; teal fights guest accents and working-cyan. With four saturated persona accents in play, any hue chosen for the most important state loses somewhere. So speaking is achromatic warm-white: brighter surface, white lamp, white ring, white transport. It reads across the room and works identically behind every character.

The discipline that makes it work comes from the other side of the argument: one glow per screen. If only the speaking thing may glow, the glow doesn't need a hue to win.

Strips, not tiles — sorted by state

Three boards used a grid of roughly-equal cards. A grid makes six agents look equally important; one is speaking, one is waiting on you, four are noise. Full-width strips give the wall a reading order (on air → asking → hand up → working → idle → stale), let the on-air channel physically expand for the caption and talkback, and put "asking" — the state that blocks an agent — above "hand up," which merely wants attention. The top slot is frozen so the wall never reshuffles under your cursor mid-click.

Broadcast metaphor: in the chrome, not the copy

The metaphor earns "on air," "hold the room," and the talkback bar — vocabulary that maps to real behavior. It does not earn "transmissions," "control booth," or talent-agency copy; one board went that loud and the whimsy starts to tax daily use. The characters carry the personality ("Dist is rebuilt, dude"), so the chrome can stay deadpan. Rule: nouns from the studio, sentences from the turtles.

The interpreter plan is a receipt in the thread

Contested between a standalone interpreter screen and an inline thread object. Decided: both, same component — the plan is a durable message (heard-utterance verbatim → numbered steps → mandatory outcome line each → one Undo) that renders inline in chat and accumulates in an Interpreter History. Never a toast. Destructive steps stop the plan and ask inline; deferred steps ("resume the room when playback ends · auto") let plans clean up after themselves; and replays carry a spend breadcrumb ("from cache, no spend") because this product bills per sentence.

PTT: white mic, two-beat arm

A held grant is a mic event, not a persona event — white ring, "MIC LIVE," everything else dims to 45%. One board colored the grant with the persona; charming, but it re-uses identity color for a state, breaking the system's one law. The persona's name in the label ("talking to donnie") already says who hears you. Borrowed from that board anyway: the ~600ms fill before the mic opens — hold-to-arm kills accidental grants, and drag-off cancels. Same affordance at three sizes: full-width bar (strip, dock), 62–88px round key (phone).

Warm room, quiet motion, visible cost

The surfaces are aubergine-tinted charcoal, not blue-black — this thing lives in a dev's periphery all night and should feel like a den, not a terminal. Motion budget: lamps breathe, the meter dances, the grant ring fills; nothing else moves, so the avatar's 70ms frame swap is never competing. And cost is visible quietly, everywhere a replay could have billed: one mono line, never a warning banner.

What's cut

  • The separate room transport bar. Transport belongs to a channel: on-air strip, dock, player. Three places you're already looking.
  • "Replay slower" as a peer button on phone. One press of the speed ToggleGroup; it survives only on desktop/dock where there's no speed UI.
  • Status-info as a permanent button. Long-press / ⋯ flips the strip to uptime · model · cwd.
  • A "current mode" label. Mode is what's lit: hazard stripes = held, cyan pill = live. A word that says "normal" earns nothing.
  • Hide-agent as a thumb-level phone action. Rare → ⋯ menu; Chat takes the slot.

What's added

  • Cue as a first-class room object. One number in the bar; "Catch up" plays everything unheard, oldest first, at 1.25×, karaoke sheet open.
  • Superseded counts on the strip, not buried — if you only heard the last of three turns, the wall says so. Queue trust is a real issue.
  • Two-beat grant arming everywhere PTT exists — no accidental hot mics, one muscle memory.
  • Spend breadcrumbs anywhere a replay could have cost money.
  • Deferred interpreter steps — "pause then replay" resumes the room by itself and says it will.
