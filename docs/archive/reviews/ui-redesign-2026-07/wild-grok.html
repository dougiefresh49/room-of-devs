Concept · Room of Devs

Footlights — the room lives in the margin

Thesis: Stop building a place you visit. Build an edge of light that rides under every other app — a theater’s footlights — where each character is a warm slot that brightens when they speak and blinks when they need you. Audio is the show. Sight is attribution, interruption, and decision. The full “stage” only opens when a turtle is waiting in the wings.

How attention works

Three states. Almost never a dashboard.

1 · Drift

Agents talk while you code. You hear them. Footlights pulse. No window. No glance required.

2 · Glance

A caption floats above the strip for the line being spoken. Enough to credit the voice without leaving the IDE.

3 · Intrude

Someone needs a decision or has finished and waits. Their slot goes HOLD. Stage opens — or the phone fills with one question.

Rationale

Why this, not another wall of persona cards.

  1. Audio-first means the UI should feel guilty taking space. Footlights are a confession that most of the day you shouldn’t look. Cards pretend equal visual weight for every agent; sound doesn’t work that way.
  2. HOLD is the product’s only “notification.” Working and speaking stay peripheral. Waiting for a human is the one time the room is allowed to be rude — blink, then intrude with one question.
  3. The Stage is a call sheet because sequences matter more than status. You care who just spoke, who’s blocked on you, and what to say next — not a grid of identical tiles.
  4. Phone = spotlight, not dashboard. One-handed use collapses to one face, one line, one thumb action. Couch time is listening and deciding, not managing a roster.
  5. Voice commands become visible plans. “Pause, then replay Donnie” isn’t a chat message — it’s a three-step strip you can cancel mid-flight. Sight serves the ear’s intentions.

Build the margin, not the room. Let the turtles earn the center of the screen.
