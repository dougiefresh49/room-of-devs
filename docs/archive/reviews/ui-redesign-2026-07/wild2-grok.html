Concept board · Room of Devs

The room is a radio play you can see.

Thesis: Stop building a chat app that happens to speak. Build a directed ensemble — four characters on a stage, audio as the live feed, UI as the glance layer. You conduct with cards and voice plans; they perform with lip-synced presence, polite waits, and huddles when the work needs more than one mind.

01 · Mac Stage

The apron, not the inbox

Characters sit on the apron with state you can read in under a second: speaking, held, decide, bench. Spoken lines fade like radio — not a forever chat scroll.

  • Lip-sync swaps avatar frames while audio plays
  • Voice plans land as cyan chips, then execute as little steps
  • Typed reply + attachment always available (headphones / silent room)
  • Decision-needed steps forward; done agents wait on the bench — no toast spam
02 · Whisper Bar

Always visible. Never steals focus.

A translucent strip over whatever you’re in. Click-through except a small grip. Shows who’s talking, a scrap of the line, and a one-tap expand to Stage.

  • Amber pip when someone joins the bench
  • Coral pip when a decision is blocking
  • Scroll the grip to scrub / replay last message
  • Hold ⌘+1…4 here too — same card model

03 · Phone · Director’s remote

One hand. Bottom third is sacred.

Phone is not a tiny Stage. It’s listen → decide → talk. Big now-playing character, thumb-reach hold-to-talk, and three actions that matter from the couch.

  • Start new agents without opening a laptop
  • Same attachments + typed replies as Mac
  • Huddle kicks a multi-voice call with characters already in the room
  • Swipe between characters instead of hunting threads
04 · The Huddle

One conversation. Many voices.

When you say “huddle” or tap it, characters rearrange into a circle with you at the center. They build on each other — overlapping speech is visible as layered waveforms, not four separate transcripts.

  • Leo can interrupt to reframe; Raph challenges; Donnie diagrams
  • You mute the room or pull one character aside mid-huddle
  • Ending a huddle leaves a single shared summary spoken by Leo

05 · Glance & Sketch

As natural as turning the laptop

Screen awareness is a timed, consentful glance — not ambient surveillance. Drawing is a shared table that drops into the Stage or Huddle: agents sketch, you scribble back, optionally over the glance.

  • “Look at this error” → consent chip → 8-second capture to that agent
  • Sketch table can start from a blank or from the glance frame
  • Both directions: Donnie draws the flow; you mark the branch you want
06 · Holding a card

Addressing is physical, not navigational

You don’t open Donnie’s thread. You hold Donnie. Keyboard card, phone press, or voice (“hey Donnie”). Release sends. That’s the atomic unit of talking back.

Rationale

Opinionated choices — what we refused, and why the four futures sit in the structure.

Why this shape

Audio owns attention

If the UI competes with the editor, the product fails. The Stage is for glances and decisions; the Whisper Bar is the always-on ear; everything else is summoned.

Cards beat threads

Multi-agent chat UIs collapse into tab hell. Holding a character is faster than finding a pane, and it maps cleanly to voice commands and phone thumb holds.

Bench > notifications

Agents that finish wait politely. Agents that block step to the apron in coral. The developer chooses when to look — the room doesn’t nag.

Huddle is structural

Group call isn’t a meeting link bolted on. It’s the same cast rearranging — which makes screen glances and sketch tables make sense as shared objects in the circle.

Phone = remote, not clone

Couch use is listen, unblock, start, huddle. Mirroring the Mac Stage would fight one-handed use. The thumb zone is the product on mobile.

Characters earn their keep

Initials + color + voice + lip-sync frames are enough identity to care. Playful cast, serious states. The TMNT framing makes “who’s talking” memorable at a glance.
