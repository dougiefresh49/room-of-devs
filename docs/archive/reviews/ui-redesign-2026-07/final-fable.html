Room of Devs · final synthesis concept

THE DEN.
One floor, one tape, one table.

The developer hears this product; the screens exist to answer what the ears can't hold. Three nouns do all the structural work. The Floor governs who may speak — one voice at a time, politely queued, and your own mic is a literal inversion of the light. The Tape is everything the room ever said, one scrubbable stream — replay, catch-up, and history are all the same gesture: seek. The Table is everything the room ever showed — a screen glance, a sketched napkin, a pasted file — which makes the four "near-future" features one feature: an object entering the conversation.

THE FLOOR

Who may speak

One voice on air, lit by achromatic light. Finished agents raise a hand and wait on a politeness ladder. Your mic inverts the surface — output puts light on a card; input turns the card into light. A group call is just the floor passed among three voices and you.

THE TAPE

What was said

Every utterance, receipt, glance, and sketch is a moment on one time-ordered tape. Away-time draws as striped debt; "catch up" pays it down oldest-first at 1.25×. A per-agent "thread" is a filter on the tape — never a separate place, which is what keeps the group call possible.

THE TABLE

What is being shown

One shared surface in the middle of the room. Toss a glance of your screen onto it, let Donnie sketch on it while he talks, paste a file from the couch. Both directions, every surface — and every object pins to the tape moment that explained it.

Provenance, in one line: the state discipline and receipts of the synthesis round (achromatic ON AIR, talkback inversion, density ladder, confirm-as-a-sentence), the social physics of open round 1 (the Tape, the politeness ladder, sound tokens, the walkie phone), and the object model of open round 2 (the Table, the Napkin, the consentful Glance, the three-voice Huddle) — fused so the near-future features are load-bearing walls, not extensions.

Nothing else in the product ever glows or inverts.

10 / RATIONALE & PROVENANCE

What won, what lost, and why.

The four futures are one feature

Screen awareness, drawing, attachments, and pastes are all an object entering the conversation. Give the room one place for objects — the Table, with a shelf and tape-pinning — and all four ship as one surface with different sources. This was round 2's single best structural idea and it anchors the whole concept.

The group call is why there are no threads

You can't bolt a multi-voice call onto per-agent threads — you'd have to invent a merge. So the Den has one tape; a “thread” is a persona filter on it, and a huddle is the floor passed among more voices. The expensive architectural decision is made up front, on purpose.

Light is the audio axis

Output puts light on a card (ON AIR); input turns the surface into light (your mic, your ink). Nothing else glows or inverts, ever. This one law survives four saturated personas, a 44px dock sliver, peripheral vision, and colorblindness — and it extends cleanly to authorship on the napkin.

Designed for the miss

The honest failure mode of an audio tool is “I wasn't listening.” The Tape draws away-time as striped debt, catch-up pays it down oldest-first at 1.25×, scrubbing is the replay feature, and the politeness ladder means nothing ever nags twice at the same volume. Silence is a designed state, because annoyance gets the whole product muted in week two.

Trust is a receipt

Every spoken command renders verbatim with per-step outcomes, destructive gates that name their cost, and an undoable history. Every replay says whether it spent money. Every glance is framed, time-boxed, logged, and expiring. The product asks for a lot of ambient trust; it pays for it in receipts.

Playfulness lives in behavior

Entrance stings, throat-clears, hands up, huddle manners, “Send him in ↵” — the characters are charming because of timing and voice, not costume chrome. Nouns from the studio, sentences from the turtles; the metaphor budget is spent on ON AIR and nowhere else.
